## What the original UI looked like

The learner product used a light cream background, royal-blue primary accent, compact top navigation, white cards with thin borders, and a floating bottom tab bar. Feature pages included Home/Foundation, Vocabulary, Grammar, Test-oriented cards, and a Partner Space section. Partner/creator pages used larger editorial typography and card sections.

## Original problems captured in the screenshots

- Floating bottom navigation overlapped page content and cards.
- Pages were visually too similar, making major features hard to distinguish at a glance.
- Weak hierarchy between page titles, supporting copy, actions, and card groups.
- Large amounts of unused horizontal space on desktop.
- Repetitive white-card treatment made the interface feel like a prototype.
- Inconsistent spacing and card density between Home, Vocabulary, Grammar, and Partner Space.
- The royal-blue + cream palette lacked enough depth or secondary accents.
- Partner/creator surfaces felt like a separate product rather than part of Hallium.
- Navigation did not scale naturally as more features were added.
- Korean content was present, but the brand identity did not strongly use Hangul as a visual element.
- Some pages relied on small text and weak contrast for secondary information.
- Repeated floating UI elements competed with learning content.

## Redesign direction selected

- Both public landing experience and signed-in app should feel like one system.
- Navigation: persistent left sidebar on desktop; compact top/mobile navigation on small screens.
- Mood: calm/editorial + warm/tactile.
- Base: warm off-white / paper tones.
- Accent family: muted lavender, rose, sage, and warm coral rather than saturated royal blue.
- Korean typography: balanced; English leads while Hangul is used deliberately for identity and content.
- Avoid: neon, dark-blue dashboard styling, excessive gradients, glassmorphism, oversized generic cards, and generic AI-site visual patterns.

This document exists specifically so the visual starting point is never lost even as the implementation evolves.
